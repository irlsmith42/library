Voice-to-text entry, later edited for clarity

Hello journal this is a voice to text written while I am driving my car home from my very first class of my English Master's degree. I was at the 19th Century American Lit class that I was on the waitlist for. The professor sent me a permission code after class, so I think I will take it. We are reading Moby Dick. Hell yes. There were a few impressions from today that I need to record even though it is too late in the day for me to do a journal entry. My brain is squeezed out like a thing that has been squeezed out and can no longer think of similes.

Impression Number 1: Cafeteria, beginning-of-the-year meeting wherein we receive civil rights and health information every year. Warm and beautiful day outside. Shady in the cafeteria. All the windows were open. A dragonfly flew into the caf during the nurse’s presentation. The nurse's voice is very quiet and hard to hear. The dragonfly flew around low, like a drone. Looking around, trying to figure out where it was and how to get out, I assume. Although it was flying so low that I mostly couldn't see it among the tables and the people, I could consistently track its motion by the people turning to look at it, and their heads turning to watch it fly past, with the bemused look. I wished to help it out to get outside. But it never came close enough to me. Later, when we were doing individual table meetings, Aliza stood near a window, and watched it go by her once or twice, but not out the window. Then a spider fell on her. That was a surprise.

Impression Number 2: During the first meeting of this class, I was not terribly impressed by my classmates’ discussion offerings. But I liked my professor a lot. During the Emerson reading (Self Reliance), we talked about how the family, according to Emerson, is one barrier to self-reliance. Some students took umbrage with this, on the level of “He's a hypocrite for saying this.” Further, he sounds like a red pill reactionary modern-day conservative guy. And it's selfish. All of these things are true, but it's literature class, not history—we're not talking about the man Emerson... That wasn't the point of this paragraph. I just have to keep talking so that the voice-to-text doesn't forget about me. Kind of like that Verizon chat I was on yesterday while my phone was dying. Anyway... The professor was like, ‘What could he mean by this? What is he talking about, about family preventing true self-reliance? Let's look at this page...’ And I said to myself, I know what Dad would say about this. In fact, I almost gave him (Dad) as an example and said, ‘You may have a family that you need to take care of, and so decide to get a day job instead of trying to be a writer,’ then the Prof took us to another page and said, ‘It was about consistency.’ Oh, consistency? Of ‘little minds’ fame? The professor was talking, so I didn't interrupt to tell my family anecdote about how that exchange of dialogue was the first thing my parents ever said to each other. This has some interesting implications for the question about self-reliance and family: Man conversing about Emerson, self-reliance king → leads to man getting married, thereby reducing man’s total amount of self-reliance; yet also makes his life better, more livable, hopefully?, and contributes presumably to man’s decision ultimately to not kill himself. Which I just found out he almost did, from Mom in the car this weekend. So.??

Final impression of the day, Impression Number 3: Of course here I am (in this uniform-ish pantsuit sort of thing...) at UMass Boston, (thinking oooooof the art teacher...) wondering if *i** is remembered here. My prof said she's been working there for 15 years, and automatically my mind started doing math, guessing whether he was here at that time... Later, as I was driving out, after dark, there were only a few people still out walking, and I saw someone very tall with short brown hair walking by... No way could it be him, I thought, he has a wife and children and a demanding job, but nonetheless I drove on into the rotary, went all the way around, and drove back to look. Obviously, it was not him.

Recently, I figured out the last sentence of this project. It’s a good one. Whenever I can discover the concluding sentence, it greatly strengthens my belief that whatever the project is, it can be completed. Not sure if this paragraph should be included in it [the project].
