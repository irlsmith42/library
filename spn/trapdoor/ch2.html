The word, finally spoken, provoked a hollow, involuntary laugh from Dean. He didn’t reply.

Cas closed his eyes, having obvious difficulty marshaling his thoughts. “I know it can be intense. I tried—to be as careful as possible. Even so, I know it can feel... the loss of control...”

Dean’s silence withered the speech, wherever it was meant to go. Enough with the euphemisms, Dean wanted to snap at him—but what would replace them? He didn’t want to find out.

“I never should have let you do it,” Dean said after a silent pause.

“I was trying to help, Dean—I did it to save you,” Cas said with feeling. “Michael was going to take possession of you. And then he would have killed me, and Jack, and your mother, and Sam. We had no other way of getting out of there.”

“We would have found another way,” Dean said, but Cas cut him off again:

“No!” he said. “No, we wouldn’t. Because you—”

He opened his mouth, then closed it.

“What?” said Dean sharply. “Because I what?”

“Because you were going to say yes!” Cas forced the words out with obvious effort. “You were going to say yes to Michael, Dean, I could see it.”

Dean’s mouth opened. “What?” he said. “No I wasn’t. I would never do that.”

Cas’s eyes were closed defensively again. “Michael, he had us. You know he did.”

Dean’s mouth was open as he searched for something to say in his defense. Nothing came to him. He shut his mouth, setting his jaw.

“Right,” he said, icy. “Right. I couldn’t handle it. You had to step in, with another one of your genius plans.”

“There was no other way,” Cas insisted. “If you hadn’t let me do it, Michael would have taken you, and then he would have killed me.”

“Then I should have let him!” Dean shouted.

Cas blinked again—he looked completely disarmed, like Dean had slapped him. It didn’t make Dean want to take it back. His gut twisted. It made him angrier.

“You don’t mean that,” Cas said.

Dean was covering his eyes, rubbing a hand down his face. A hollow laugh came out his mouth. “You’re right. I should have just let him possess me. That’s what I was made for, right? It’s all I’m good for, isn’t it?”

He expected a ‘no’ to that, but instead, Cas said, voice harder, “Maybe you should have. I was just trying to get us to safety. And in case you’ve forgotten, it worked.”

“Right. Right,” said Dean again, eyes still closed. “You were right. You’re always right.”

He dropped his hand, and faced Cas in the silent hallway. His coloring was weird, blotchy. Maybe it was the lights down here. It almost looked like he was sweating.

Dean turned and opened his bedroom door. The click was so loud in the hall that it startled him. He hit the light switch, and strode to his bed, where he threw his bag down on it and unzipped it, just for something to do with his hands. He started taking things out, throwing them onto the bedspread. He thought if Cas tried to follow him in here, he'd hit him, he really would.

After a moment, he glanced over his shoulder. Cas had moved into the doorway. He hovered on the threshold, without coming in.

As Dean looked back, his eyes moved over the empty spot next to his bed again. “Bet you liked that, huh?” he said, looking back at his duffel. He had no idea what he was unpacking; he didn’t even know what he was holding in his hands. A knife. He threw it down onto the bed. “Possessing Michael’s sword. You probably rubbed it in his face.”

Dean turned around. Cas’s face looked worse, even more drawn, like he was about to be sick.

“Dean,” said Cas, hoarsely. “If you really think that I—” He broke off, then reset. “It wasn’t like that.”

No, Dean thought. It wasn’t.

Dean stared down at the buttons on Cas’s coat.

When he finally looked up, Cas was staring right at him.

“What are you, reading my mind?” he snapped.

“No!”

“You stay the hell out of there.”

Dean threw down the shirt he’d been holding and strode across the room towards the door. “I could hit you right now,” he snarled. “Maybe I should—”

He reached Cas with his fist pulled back, but before he could throw a punch, Cas’s hand shot out and caught his wrist.

His expression was hard—Dean pushed his arm forward with a savage, stubborn jerk, and Cas shoved back, wrenching Dean’s fist away from his face. Cas’s mouth twitched angrily, and resolved into a thin line.

“Dean!” he said sharply. "Stop."

Dean’s breath was coming hard and fast.

“Whatever it takes,” said Cas, voice strained. “You’re the one who always says we do whatever it takes. That’s what I did. That’s what we did.” Cas said it steadily, trying to keep calm, but by the end of his speech, his voice wavered.

"So you don't regret it?" said Dean, his wrist still trapped in Cas’s grip.

"No!" said Cas.

"And you're not gonna say I'm sorry."

"No. I'm not. It worked. I got you away from Michael, and we got everyone home safely."

"And that's it."

Take it back, Dean thought. Say you never wanted it. If you’re reading thoughts, read that thought.

But Cas didn't.

"What's it?" he said instead.

Dean clenched his jaw, tensing—instinct was telling him to escalate, yank his trapped right hand back and then catch Cas with a surprise left hook—but he couldn’t stand the way Cas was looking at him. Not for another second.

“Dean.”

He did yank his arm back, and Cas let go, holding up open hands in truce.

“Get out,” Dean said.

“What?” said Cas, more affronted than when Dean had tried to hit him.

“Get out of here! Out of the bunker, Cas! You need to go.”

“I—” Cas broke off, mouth open— “No,” he said. His voice was firm, but loud, and Dean could tell it was to keep it from quivering. “Jack needs me.”

He shut his mouth tightly.

Dean stared at him for another second. “Fine,” he said. “In that case, I’m outta here.” He turned without another word and returned to the bed. He shoved his things back into his bag and, without zipping it, slung it over his shoulder. When he turned to leave, Cas was still standing in the doorway.

“Where are you going?” he asked.

“Get the hell out of my way."

Cas moved aside, quickly enough not to touch him. He shoved past Cas and out of his room.

"Don't call," Dean added.

Cas stood at Dean's doorway, watching him disappear down the hallway, back towards the garage. A moment later, the door slammed shut.
