DVD Commentary

Jump to:

Hello. Welcome to my DVD commentary on The Trapdoor. I am an English teacher by trade, so the purpose of this page is to do my own English homework for your enjoyment. By which I mean, show my work, and hopefully one day you can apply one of these approaches to a writing problem you're facing. Supernatural is a very rich text, by which I do not mean well-written or narratively complex, but as A Text, it is rich. There is a lot going on inside it and almost as much going on outside of it, around it, and in response to it. This story is weird and meta and fourth-wall-breaky because Supernatural is that way. That multi-media-meta-whatever-it-is makes it perfectly suited for a long, overly involved, web-based-multimedia-meta story, and that happens to be one of my favorite types of art.

This is also kind of spn criticism, which I haven't really written before [I just say it to my friend B on Discord voice chat]. Trapdoor is a highly adversarial text, as you probably noticed. So, to explain structural decisions, I will also explain what aspect of Supernatural was pissing me off at the time.

The #1 thing you need to know about this fic is that I Fucking Love The X-Files. One of the potent ingredients for the 'Getting Leo Addicted to Supernatural' Potion is its intertextualities with the X-Files. Writers and directors and actors worked on both shows, and many spn episodes are carbon copies of XF episodes. From a fandom perspective, Mulder and Scully, though not queerbait [technically], through their decade of UST, established a foundation for the queerbait era. They also originated many of the fanfic tropes and paradigms for SFF Will-They-or-Won't-They-Couples—a grand tradition that I believe Dean and Cas were, to be honest, the last. So I, wishing to participate in that intertextual conversation and also amuse myself, wove a lot of XF stuff into Trapdoor.

Chapter 1

13x12 "Sign, Seal, Deliver"

In chapters 1-4, Dean is dealing with post-possession dissociation and disorientation. Mostly, this is in dreams--except for this opening scene. He experiences identity confusion, doesn't realize he's wearing the coat but also won't take it off, and is generally lost in his own body and brain. This is deeply influenced by DCWT, which is 250K words exploring this fascinating post-Drift state, i.e. what might result from briefly syncing brains with your bestie. [And an alien hivemind. But that's not important right now.] Dean does not experience nearly as much "personality bleed" as Newt & Hermann in DCWT, because it's a different mechanism, and Dean & Cas's minds did not "sync." But their minds did *touch*. Which isn't good if you're Cas, and deeply in love with Dean in a deeply horny and homosexual way, and do NOT want him to know that.

And it's not good if you're Dean, either, because you did not want to know that. Additionally, you are dealing with the abrupt end of your Widower Arc, during which you got closer to rock bottom than ever before. Now that Cas is OK, you don't have to think about that again, right? Problem solved, right? No need to interrogate it or evaluate it. Not like it could happen *again*. LOL.

So, Dean is Not Thinking About It.

His list of things to not think about is almost getting too long to drink about.

Originally I watched all of the existent Supernatural in 2013 [season 9], and then became fed up with it by 2014 [season 10] and rage quit. One thing I found more and more creepy as the show went on was the way they talked about "family." It was always in some incredibly grim context, like asking for a major sacrifice, or trying to do a big no homo, and it never actually made any sense. Motherfucker, I know you do not think of Kevin as your family. So why are you saying that? I felt like the show was trying to brainwash me, or indoctrinate me into something. But indoctrinate me into what???

As the years went by, I learned more about this world. As a result of my education I began to understand Supernatural as an expression of American cultural hegemony. So whenver they talked about "family," I mentally substituted the word "America," to see if that made more sense. They weren't trying to indoctrinate me into some weird little cult--they were trying to indoctrinate me into the regular cult. The big one. The same cult as every other show, sports game, and commercial on TV. Riiiiight, that one. Yawn.

What's most repellent to me about this is that the people who make the show seem to genuinely believe in this mission and unironically embrace all of the Americanisms embodied by "Dean." Masculinity is a huuuge portion of this, of course, because the perfect American is a man.

Much has been written about this topic in relation to Dean [lady i'm judith butler], and I wanted to add to that converation by making all the Dean foils in this story women: Bobbie, Claire, Melinda, Jean, and the 'wife' Dean makes up when he tells Darren his fake life story.

and then quit within 2 years, because when they killed Kevin and then Charlie, I realized what I had already known, but tried to ignore: The ppl making the show genuinely did not like me, nerdy former AP student and gay female fan, and they were either happy or indifferent about killing my in-show avatars, because they just did not actually give a fuck about me. That's no way to live, so I quit, went on the hero's journey, and returned home [Supernatural] changed [stronger, gayer], in 2020, to incur the incredible mental damage awaiting me [and you].

14x02 "The Spider Who Came In From the Cold"

At this point, we’re getting serious about uninstalling the Kripke/Dabb/Chuck-esque restrictions and living in the ‘real’ world. Things Sam & Dean never deal with onscreen, that they deal with here:

- Everyday interactions w Lebanon community (grocery store; see next chapter for more grocery store)

- Seasons, the time of year (Valentine’s Day decorations, blizzards), and, by extension, the passage of time

- Canada (?!??!? despite being filmed there? And our inextricably linked highway systems??)

The chilling absence of Canada in the series is I would say as conspicuous an absence-via-presence as the gay sex is. What I’m saying is this:

filming in Canada : never acknowledging Canada onscreen :: gay subtext : 15x18 gay love confession & immediate death

And so what I’m saying is this:

So in this episode/chapter, we are going to Canada. In the tradition of Steve Yockey (patron saint, unbeknownst to him, of this project), who wrote “Celebrating the Life of Asa Fox,” in which he puts an end to the ‘no Canada’ rule (as well as the equally pernicious setting-flattener, the ‘no community of hunters’ rule of the post-Roadhouse era, thank you, Steve).

And, before we do, trunk shot but for something indisputably non-badass. I put this in whenever possible because I think it’s funny and I always take an opportunity to disrespect The Institution of The Impala.

And now, Sam and Dean driving scene. Mirror of the scene at the beginning of Ch 3, where Sam is very stressed to be in the car w Dean [driving angry, and tailgating, etc.] so he keeps telling Dean to slow down... and then Dean hits the car in front of him anyway. This Dean Winchester behavior I invented* [*lifted from a pivotal incident in my personal life] is also featured in “The River,” where Dean permanently messes up Cas’s car by doing this. Wishing to be careless about himself → carelessness with his family members, whom he considers non-autonomous parts of himself; Carelessness + anger at self → actively self-destructive behaviors, such as driving, drinking, or both.

In this chapter, Dean is behaving strangely, but not angrily—because he quit drinking—so he still drives unsafely—but not angrily—and when they get into the accident, it is minimally destructive—and Dean doesn’t get upset about it.

Sam reflects on all of these strange changes [including Dean’s withdrawal sickness, he just thinks it was the flu] with growing alarm. He doesn't know why Dean is acting differently, because he doesn't know Dean quit.

In chapter 3, while freaking out about the car accident, Sam points out to Dean that they have no car insurance. Now, in chapter 9, Sam brings up insurance and Dean informs him that he has AAA. Sam mishears and thinks he said AA. This is true, too, but not what Dean said aloud. So, The car : the home :: driving : domestic policy. Drinking : no thoughts of the future :: AA : AAA. Layers of communication. Dean not saying what should be said, but It still being Said. See the next episode, obv, for more of that.

One last narrative/Winchesters’-real-life rule to disrespect—all the stuff on this road trip occurs between act 1 and act 2, i.e. it is not part of the “aired” version, i.e. the scripted version of their life. And since this part does not ‘air,’ Dean is able to choose whether or not to kill the supernatural creature. Because of his reorientation on the Jack issue, he lets Sam let them go.

Hotel! Ugly-ass chain hotel! You know how i feel about these. The Real 2010s America.

14x03 "The Dean Winchester Show"

YAY! This is my favorite episode in the whole fic. I really let my hair down on this one and got silly. Of all the fake episodes in Trapdoor, this is the one I most desperately want to watch. In the multi-year process of writing this fic, I developed a lot as a writer, so now when I look back, I see that the complexity is uneven, and I fear that in the early chapters, some of the script-to-prose format switches are not sufficiently justified. But in this episode, dude, I think I finally got it. Instead of mashing adjacent ideas next to each other, this episode layers them on top of one another in a way I find conceptually delightful. Especially because some of these ideas are just stupid as fuuuuck.

1. Setting: Bottle episode (almost). In a classic “Supernatural motel room” set, a la ‘Changing Channels.’ By this allusion, this announces to the discerning viewer that this will be an episode About the show. But this tiny, static set cannot contain them, and it is, eventually, revealed to be illusory—because at this point in the story, they are no longer meaningfully bound by the Kripkean narrative.

2. Format: Typed like a stage play script. This is an IRL-Ao3-audience facing decision, to visually remind the reader that we are in a new medium. Also, fundamentally, it's an episode about PERFORMING, which is most iconically done on a STAGE. [Andrew Dabb winks at you and it makes a DING sound]

3. Television Show: Bottle episode + stage play = I Love Lucy-style audience, laughter, and ‘bumbling husband’ plot. At times, Dean appears to react to or be aware of the audience, because he is.

4. Plot: So obviously the aphasia hex is a reworking of ‘Regarding Dean,’ an episode that is funny for a few reasons, but tragically misses a lot of opportunities. In Chapter 9, Dean gets this hex at this particular moment bc he is changing on the inside (sobriety, un-repressing some things), and this change will, for a time, render him incomprehensible/unrecognizable to his loved ones. Unless he chooses to communicate with them about it. Additionally, the hex gives him the opportunity to say some of The Things he’s been Not Saying, now that he’s in a place to process them. Like, “Cas, you’re gay,” and so on. [Favorite sentence I've written in this fic?]

5. Canada: See above.

6. Tacky CW Feminism: MERLIN WAS A WOMAAAAN, DUHHHH!!!! The corny-ass concept of 'Merlin but a sexy woman' is so Supernatural-ish that it cracks me uppp. It just seems like the kind of stupid-ass speech Yockey or Berens would write early in the 1st Trump presidency.

Chapter 10

14x04 "Payback's a Bitch"

If we are really serious about getting out of the kripke-dabb-paradigm, then we are going to watch a monster POV episode where they are sympathetic, something that I (once again) CANNOT believe they only did one time. This one has ghouls, yay, and it is “written” by Davy Perez because he is a paradigm-interrogator. [The quaint+twee ‘goth ghouls working at small-town funeral home’ setup is pretty Yockeyian, I admit, but he wrote the previous one, so it doesn't make level-two-universe sense.]

The idea is: from another narrative point of view, the story of the Winchesters is completely different, which should call into question the main narrative presented to you, and make you ask, ‘is this propaganda?’ This is actually a pretty basic type of episode to have in your long-running show, most long-running cult shows do at least one like this—even It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia has these, for God's sake—and yet, Supernatural never does, because it is so earnestly in love with its own premise. Kripke-Gamble-Carver-Dabb-etc. act like it's cringe but they allllll believe in this premise wholeheartedly.

So, ghouls. Goth ghouls :3 The ghouls are named after iconic X-Files villains:

Jean Tooms = Eugene Tooms (‘Squeeze’, ‘Tooms’)

Patrice Modell = Patrick Modell (‘Pusher’, ‘Kitsunegari’)

Luther Broggs = Luther Lee Boggs (‘Beyond the Sea’)

Dwayne Berry = Duane Barry (‘Duae Barry,’ ‘Ascension’) I feel a little bad calling him a villain, he’s not a "bad guy."" But he is an antagonist, and he causes Scully to get abducted by aliens, which will weigh against him on the scales of Anubis.

As with Ch 9, I think this one is cohesive, and I like that. However, stepping back, I also have some philosophical qualms about its cohesiveness, because that in itself is narrative artifice, which we’re supposed to be pulling away from at this point... The counterargument/justification to that is that this cohesive story is not about those narrative fugitives, the Winchesters, and they are secondary characters in this narrative. The implication is that they are maybe actually just a small part of a larger world, despite what Kripke-Gamble-Carver-Dabb would have you think.

The other implication, which I wish Part 2 to at all times by implying, is that their lives are continuing offscreen. So this chapter's focus on other characters also reinforces that. [i.e. they, like us, go through time periods where they know nothing about the Winchesters' lives. Can you imagine if such a thing were possible?]

Another purpose of this story is to obliquely tell the story of the widower arc—the missing months while Cas was dead and Mary&Jack were missing. That period of time only gets shown obliquely in Trapdoor, because (1) it’s a major part of what Dean is repressing, so we do not get access, and (2) he was drinking a lot and actually doesn’t remember a lot of it. Also (3) John Le Carré says to always start the story as late as you can.

Now, however, that we are post-blackberries, and post-sobriety, we (us+Dean) get a quick look. [and by that i now *just* mean the readers, not the hypothetical TV audience. They are losing access.] We’re not going to get the whole story, bc sobriety and because of the aforementioned memory loss [and (3) Ibid]. Instead, the Dean role will be played by our new friend Jean [woman]. We see the story of her widow arc. Different situation, but they have comparable internal reactions, guilts, anxieties, repressions, etc.

Another narrative rule that is ignored in this episode is acts. This episode has no commercial breaks.

Re: above mentioned 14x02 grocery store / interactions w the Lebanon community at large. Though the Winchesters are peripheral in this episode, their presence in the community is acknowledged and explored in the 'investigating the town' montage. The rumors, stories, and general impression of them is very muddled, bc I think that’s what it would be, realistically. People are nosy, yes, but generally extremely uninformed. And yes, the Winchesters would be most known at the gun store. And yes, if they thought anything of it, they would think Mary was their sister. All of this should be taken as further proof that the Winchesters are becoming more cosmically insignificant.
